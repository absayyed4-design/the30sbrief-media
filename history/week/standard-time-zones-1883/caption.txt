The day America heard noon twice 🕛 📜

On November 18, 1883, American and Canadian railroads switched to standard time zones. Before that, towns set clocks by the sun, with more than 300 local times in use. At standard noon, station clocks were reset, so many places saw noon twice, which is why it is called the Day of Two Noons. It was a railroad plan, and Congress did not make time zones law until the Standard Time Act of 1918.
